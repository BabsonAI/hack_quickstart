Spend the clock wisely

suggested timebox · 3-hour hack

A plan beats drift. These are suggestions, not rules — but the back half is where demos are won or lost, so protect it. Times assume a ~5:15 start; shift them to match your actual start time.

⚖️  Halfway checkpoint (~6:45): have something runnable. If not, cut scope now.
  1. 5:15
    20 min · Pick & scope

    Lock the one thing you'll demo

    Decide the single thing you'll show at 8:15 and say it in one sentence. Go smaller than feels safe — one feature done beats five half-built.

  2. 5:35
    15 min · Set up

    Keys, repo, a running skeleton

    API keys, a repo, and a "hello world" that actually runs. Don't sink 40 minutes into setup — see the tools & quickstart.

  3. 5:50
    85 min · Build the core

    One path, end to end

    Get the most important path working start-to-finish before adding anything else. Lean on AI to move fast — but keep producing, not just reading.

  4. 7:15
    30 min · Make it demoable

    Feature freeze, then polish

    Stop adding. Smooth the one path you'll show, load sample data, and remove anything that can break live. A rough thing that runs beats a slick thing that doesn't.

  5. 7:45
    20 min · Prep the pitch

    Build your 2 minutes

    Slide or script. Decide who talks and who clicks. Open on the point, not the backstory. Run it once, out loud, against the clock.

  6. 8:05
    10 min · Buffer

    Save, commit, reset

    Commit and push. Reset your demo to a clean starting state. Close the noisy tabs. Breathe — things always wobble at the end.

  7. 8:15
    Hands down

    Tools down → judging & pitches

    Present what you have. Done and shown beats perfect and hidden.

Consume vs. create

the one habit that decides your demo

AI makes it dangerously easy to spend three hours reading docs, watching demos, and chatting with a model — all input, nothing to show. You're judged on what you create, not what you consumed.

Tilt toward creating

Consuming (reading, prompting, exploring) is how you learn fast — early on, lean into it. But the meter has to swing the other way before the clock runs out.

Rule of thumb: building > browsing by the halfway mark.
